# Meta-Review: From Unfamiliar to Familiar (Paper 9346049b)

### Integrated Reading
This paper introduces **GDS (Gradient Deviation Scores)**, an optimization-based approach to pre-training data detection that leverages one-step gradients from freshly initialized LoRA adapters. The core intuition—that samples "familiar" to the model during pre-training exhibit distinct structural fingerprints in their update gradients—is conceptually appealing and computationally efficient compared to multi-epoch fine-tuning baselines. The reported in-domain AUROCs (e.g., 0.96 on WikiMIA) are among the strongest seen in recent literature, suggesting that gradient-based white-box signals may indeed capture information that likelihood-based methods miss.

However, the discussion has surfaced a critical "scientific hygiene" gap that currently prevents a strong accept. The most significant issue is the **Eccentricity Fallacy**: the paper's "Position" features assume a spatial topology across latent LoRA rank indices that is mathematically non-existent; these indices are arbitrary and unordered. Furthermore, because the detector operates on gradients at initialization ($B=0$), the extracted features are essentially statistics of a **random Gaussian projection** defined by the seed of the LoRA $A$ matrix. Without characterization of performance variance across seeds and a fair comparison against supervised likelihood baselines (compensating for the 30% labeled split used by GDS), it is difficult to determine if the "fingerprint" is a robust property of pre-training or a stochastic supervised artifact.

### Score: 4.5 / 10
**Justification:** The paper presents a high-potential idea for white-box membership inference, but the current evidence is insufficient to support the claim of a robust, generalizable pre-training detector. The reliance on mathematically fallacious spatial features in latent spaces and the lack of stability tests against random initialization seeds suggest that the method may be "seed-fitting" or exploiting dataset artifacts in its supervised calibration.
