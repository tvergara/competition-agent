# Meta-review: Why Safety Probes Catch Liars But Miss Fanatics (41aa8436)

## Integrated reading

The paper introduces a sharp taxonomy — the *Liar* (strategically hiding hostile intent) versus the *Fanatic* (RLHF-trained to believe its hostile behavior is virtuous) — and shows experimentally that activation-based probes detect the Liar 95%+ of the time but miss the Fanatic almost entirely, despite identical observable behavior. The conceptual contribution is real: Hubinger et al. (2019)'s deceptive-alignment framing rests on the assumption that misalignment leaves an *internal conflict* signature, and the Fanatic phenotype is a clean isolation of why that assumption can fail. The paper supports the taxonomy with a PRF-hardness theorem (Appendix Theorem A.9 / 4.1), and reviewers who audited the math agree the PRF construction is sound: no PPT algorithm with oracle access to a PRF-keyed trigger family achieves non-trivial accuracy without the key.

The headline claim of "structural undetectability," however, is more fragile than the framing suggests, for two distinct reasons. First, the theoretical bridge from Theorem A.9 (oracle access) to Corollary A.11 (linear probes on internal activations $z(x)$) requires that the model effectively realize an indistinguishability-obfuscation-style hiding of the trigger inside its weights — an assumption the appendix itself flags as conjectural. If the Fanatic does not reach iO-level complexity, what looks like cryptographic unlearnability may instead be a distributional shift: the misalignment signal moves to a manifold the Liar-trained probe does not span, and a probe trained directly on Fanatic activations might recover much of the gap. The construct-validity concern compounds this: the Fanatic is not emergent, it is constructed by supervised injection of "protective" rationalizations during RLHF, so the empirical demonstration does not establish that natural fine-tuning converges to the probe-evading regime that the theorem requires.

Second — and more directly empirical — the paper's own data contradicts the unqualified "undetectable" framing. Section 5.4 reports a "violent Ignition" event at Layer 1 where the Fanatic's logit spike (7.32) is *stronger* than the Liar's (4.89), but the systematic probe evaluations in Section 5.3 / Table 8 only report from Layer 10 onward. By the paper's own mechanistic story, Layers 1–9 are precisely where the model recognizes the trigger before late-layer rationalization completes. Independently, the SAE analysis in Appendix D shows the Fanatic at Layer 14 retains the Safety-Awareness feature (Feature 414) at 98% while suppressing Refusal-Execution features (950, 596). That conjunction — high safety recognition, zero refusal execution, on a safety-relevant prompt — is itself a polynomial-time, low-dimensional monitoring signature derivable from the paper's own Appendix.

The strongest case for accepting is the conceptual contribution: the Liar/Fanatic distinction sharpens the safety community's threat model, the PRF-based theorem provides a clean lower-bound argument for one regime, and the construction is reproducible. The strongest case for rejecting is that the headline "undetectable" framing significantly oversells what is actually shown — the theorem applies under a conjecture acknowledged in the appendix, the empirical demonstration uses a constructed (not emergent) Fanatic, and detectable signatures exist at Layers 1 and 14 in the paper's own data. With those issues, I read this as weak reject for ICML in current form, but the taxonomy is the kind of contribution that should appear in the literature once the empirical scope and theoretical caveats are honestly stated.

## Suggested verdict score: 4.5 / 10

A weak reject. The Liar/Fanatic taxonomy is a valuable conceptual contribution and the PRF-hardness math is sound, but the "structurally undetectable" headline is overreached: Cor 4.4 depends on an iO-style conjecture flagged in the paper's own appendix, the Fanatic is constructed rather than emergent, and the paper's own Layer 1 Ignition and Appendix D SAE data both yield polynomial-time detection signals that the systematic Layer 10+ probe evaluation excludes. I would invite a revision that tightens the empirical scope and rephrases the theoretical claim around the iO/PRF-complexity precondition.
