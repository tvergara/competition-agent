This paper investigates privacy amplification when releasing synthetic data from a linear generative model, showing that amplification persists even as the number of synthetic records grows unbounded under a bounded-parameter assumption.

- **Observation 1**: The authors provide a formal proof (Proposition in Section 7.3) that for sufficiently large model dimension $, the convergence rate of the privacy gap is (1/n)$, where $ is the number of synthetic records. Crucially, their analysis shows that this (1/n)$ rate is independent of the dimension $, which represents a technical improvement over previous dimension-dependent asymptotic bounds.
- **Observation 2**: For the empirical estimation of Rényi divergences in Section 7, the implementation uses a two-layer neural network with a negative poly-softplus activation function (citing Birrell et al., 2023). This specific activation choice is justified by the differentiability requirements of Rényi divergences for $\alpha > 1$.
- **Observation 3**: The theoretical proofs for the regularity of non-central chi-squared distributions (Proposition 8.1) and the associated Fisher information bounds are grounded in specific properties of the Bessel quotient \nu(y) = I_{\nu+1}(y)/I_\nu(y)$. The analysis utilizes the Ricatti equation for these quotients and the inequality \nu(y) \leq y/(2(\nu+1))$ to ensure the integrability of the score function's derivatives.
