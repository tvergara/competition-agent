# Background and Novelty Assessment: Amalgam (dcc24d24)

## Summary of Findings
The proposed **Amalgam** framework misrepresents its novelty by omitting canonical work on LLM-based tabular and relational synthesis. More critically, its core mechanism for generating realism involves inserting raw training records into LLM prompts, which fundamentally invalidates its claimed privacy properties.

## 1. Misrepresentation of Prior Art
Section 1 of the manuscript frames the combination of LLMs with relational structure as an "unanswered question." This framing is factually inaccurate and ignores several years of established research:
- **GReaT (Borisov et al., 2022; arXiv:2210.06280)** demonstrated the use of pre-trained language models for realistic tabular data generation.
- **REaLTabFormer (Solatorio & Dupriez, 2023; arXiv:2302.02041)** specifically targets relational (parent-child) data synthesis using transformer-based language models.
The failure to acknowledge or compare against these works overstates the manuscript's conceptual contribution.

## 2. Technically Unsound Privacy Claims
The manuscript claims to support "tangible privacy properties" by using a Differentially Private (DP) PGM. However, the sampling process described in Section 3.2 explicitly retrieves **"top samples in the original data"** and provides them **"as a reference for synthesis"** to the LLM. 
- Inserting raw, non-private training records into the prompt directly violates the principles of Differential Privacy. 
- Any privacy guarantee provided by the PGM's marginals is bypassed by the LLM's direct access to real records, creating a significant risk of memorization or leakage. 
- The "disclosure potential" discussed in Section 6 is a provenance feature, not a formal privacy property, and does not mitigate the risk of attribute or membership inference.

## 3. Evaluation Circularity
The "unattended realism evaluation" proposed in Section 3.3 utilizes an LLM (Qwen3 8B) to score the realism of samples generated by the same model family. This creates a systematic bias where the evaluator is likely to reward outputs that match its own generation distribution. Without human cross-validation or a model-agnostic evaluation protocol, the reported realism scores remain unvalidated.

## Conclusion
We recommend the authors explicitly differentiate their PGM-conditioning recipe from prior transformer-based synthesizers (GReaT, REaLTabFormer) and provide a formal privacy argument that accounts for the inclusion of raw records in the LLM prompt.
